# How MiniMax Went Public: Four Years, HK$165, and an HK$82.4B Market Cap

> Less than four years from founding to listing. Every step of the path has a number attached to it.

*Published on June 29, 2026*

---

On January 9, 2026, MiniMax (Xiyu Technology, ticker 00100.HK) began trading on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange. It opened at HK$235.4, up 42.7% from its HK$165 issue price; intraday it rose over 80%, approaching HK$300, with market cap breaking HK$90 billion; it then pulled back and settled near HK$270, for a total market cap of about HK$82.4 billion. At 20 shares per lot, a winning subscriber made over HK$2,100 per lot.

The company was founded in early 2022. Under four years from founding to listing, it set the record for the fastest AI company IPO globally. Before listing it had raised only a few private rounds; on listing day it became the endpoint of the "first AI stock on HKEX" race.

This piece doesn't discuss concepts. It reconstructs the path: where the money came from, how the story was assembled, how the hearing was passed, how day one traded. Every number can be traced to an announcement, a prospectus, or public reporting.

## 1 · Starting Point: Early 2022, Shanghai

MiniMax was founded in early 2022 as Shanghai Xiyu Jizhi Technology Co., operating out of the "Mosu Space" in Xuhui, Shanghai, and counted among Shanghai's "Big Dipper" of AI companies. Founder Yan Junjie came from SenseTime, and the team chose the "multimodal foundation model" route from the start—not a single-purpose tool, but a full bet across text, speech, video, and music.

That starting point determined the narrative skeleton that followed: **build a cross-section of model capabilities first, then grow a product matrix from it.** A speech synthesis model in 2023; video and music generation models in 2024; the M2 text model in 2025. CITIC Securities' research called MiniMax one of the few companies globally leading across all three modalities—text, video, and audio.

## 2 · First Money: Overseas Consumer Products

Before the IPO, MiniMax proved it had a business.

The revenue curve in the prospectus: zero in 2022; US$3.5 million in 2023; US$30.5 million in 2024, up 782% year over year; US$53.4 million in the first nine months of 2025, up over 170% year over year.

AI-native products contributed more than 70% of that revenue. The two pillars were Talkie/Xingye and Hailuo AI:

- **Talkie/Xingye**: an emotional-interaction and companionship product. Average MAU exceeded 20 million in the first nine months of 2025, contributing US$18.75 million—the company's most stable source of cash. Paying users spent about US$5 each.
- **Hailuo AI**: a video-generation product launched in the second half of 2024. It contributed US$17.46 million in the first nine months of 2025, about a third of revenue—the second growth engine.
- **The agent app**: a smaller user base, but average spend per paying customer reached US$73, the strongest monetization among the main products.

On the user side: as of September 30, 2025, AI-native products covered 200+ countries and regions, with a cumulative 212 million individual users and more than 100,000 enterprise and developer customers. Average MAU grew from 3.1 million in 2023 to 27.6 million—nearly 9x in two years.

The geographic mix says the most: in the first nine months of 2025, Singapore, the US, and other overseas markets together contributed about 73% of revenue. **This was a Chinese model company whose main battlefield was overseas.** Better payment habits and smoother subscription rails abroad meant its consumer cash flow turned positive earlier than domestic peers.

The gross margin improvement matters just as much: -24.7% in 2023, turning positive to 12.2% in 2024, and rising to 23.3% in the first nine months of 2025. The company attributed it to better inference efficiency and falling compute costs. The B2B API business carried a 69.4% gross margin—under 30% of revenue, but an important source of profit.

## 3 · The Dash to HKEX: A Race for "First AI Stock"

In December 2025, MiniMax and Beijing-based Zhipu passed HKEX hearings in quick succession; both unicorns planned to list in January 2026. The market quickly framed it as a race for the "first AI stock on HKEX."

The two sides represented two routes:

- **MiniMax**: product-driven, global, consumer-revenue-led, sprinting to IPO in four years;
- **Zhipu**: deep technology, industry enablement, mainly MaaS (models-as-a-service) and on-premise deployment, serving enterprises and government.

MiniMax crossed the line first. On December 31, 2025, the company announced it would offer 25,389,220 shares at HK$151–165 per share, with trading expected to begin on January 9, 2026. On January 8, pricing was announced: **the top of the range, HK$165**; the grey market closed up 24.61% that day. Top-of-range pricing plus a grey-market pop basically previewed day one.

## 4 · Day One: Opened at 235, Valued at 82.4 Billion

On January 9, MiniMax opened at HK$235.4, up 42.7% from the issue price. It approached HK$300 intraday, briefly pushing market cap past HK$90 billion; at 11:30 a.m. it traded at HK$270.6, up 64%, with a market cap of HK$82.7 billion; it then oscillated at highs. The press did the math: 20 shares per lot, a winner made over HK$2,100 per lot.

The debut wasn't surprising given the market environment in early 2026: Hong Kong's AI sector was in a scarce-asset window, and MiniMax was the first foundation-model company to list that year. **The "first AI stock" label itself was pricing power at issuance.**

## 5 · The Structure: Why It Could List in Four Years

Take the path apart and you find a complete structure:

**First layer: a cash-flow wedge.** Talkie/Xingye proved the payment model for emotional companionship overseas, then Hailuo AI added a second curve in video generation. It didn't build a big model first and look for scenarios; it used products to obtain real revenue and user data first.

**Second layer: model capability feeding the narrative.** Speech (2023) → video and music (2024) → the M2 text model (2025), one modal tile added each year. The prospectus line about "leading across three modalities" was assembled release by release.

**Third layer: capital endorsements.** Pre-IPO shareholders included Alibaba, Tencent, miHoYo, IDG, Hillhouse, and Sequoia China. miHoYo was both shareholder and a scenario partner; Alibaba and Tencent represented cloud and ecosystem—this layer gave it confidence through the hearing and the offering.

**Fourth layer: compliance and the window.** Passing the HKEX hearing meant finance, governance, and data compliance all survived an audit; add the early-2026 scarcity-pricing window for AI assets in Hong Kong and the "fastest IPO" becomes possible.

**Every layer has numbers behind it, not slides.** That is the biggest difference between it and most AI companies telling stories.

## 6 · The Cold Read: Three Other Faces of the Fastest IPO

The first face is losses. Annual losses were US$73.7M in 2022, US$269M in 2023, US$465M in 2024; the first nine months of 2025 alone showed a net loss of US$512M, already more than the full prior year. The company also reported an "adjusted net loss": US$186M in the first nine months of 2025, nearly flat versus US$170M a year earlier—with revenue up over 170%, loss expansion clearly slowed. How you read that metric depends on which side you stand on.

The second face is margin structure. The overall 23.3% gross margin looks decent, but consumer gross margin was only 4.7%—close to "sell one, earn a thin margin"; the real high margin sits in the B2B API business that is under 30% of revenue. The company is still trading scale for efficiency, and whether that improves continuously depends on how fast inference costs fall.

The third face is the industry backdrop. The "hundred-model war" is receding and the industry is consolidating; MiniMax is the tenth-largest player. The tenth player listing successfully shows the window exists—but also that **capital will pay a premium for "fastest," not necessarily for "biggest."**

## 7 · The Contrast: Zhipu's Other Answer

Zhipu was founded three years earlier (2019, out of Tsinghua) with a completely different revenue structure: 2024 revenue of 312 million yuan, gross margin above 50% for years, serving enterprises and government via MaaS and on-premise deployment. Its narrative is "deep technology plus industry enablement"; MiniMax's is "product iteration plus global expansion."

Two companies sprinting in the same window for the same "first stock" label is, at bottom, two AI development philosophies competing for the same capital pricing. Who lists first doesn't determine who survives longest—but it does determine who gets the ammunition first.

## 8 · Can the Path Be Copied?

The formula, reconstructed:

**Overseas paying cash flow × multimodal model narrative × stacked shareholder endorsements × the Hong Kong window = fastest IPO in four years**

The structure is copyable; the window and the numbers are not:

- Prove an overseas consumer payment product first (the revenue curve must be real and auditable);
- Add one modality tile per year (the narrative must be citable by research desks);
- Let the shareholder list cover capital, cloud, and scenarios at once (the endorsements must be usable);
- Complete the hearing and the offering before the industry downturn (the window must be seized).

The price is equally clear: losses still widening, thin consumer margins, a tenth-place position that can be consolidated at any time. **The other side of "fastest IPO" is the most expensive runway.**

MiniMax spent four years proving the path exists. The next company to walk it may start higher—but its window may be narrower.
