# An Outline of AI Economics: How Human Society Rebuilds Value, Scarcity, and Power After Material Abundance

> Economics is the study of scarcity. When AI rewrites the distribution of scarcity, economics itself has to be rewritten.

*Published on June 3, 2026*

---

## 0 · Core Thesis

Classical economics is built on one assumption: **matter is scarce**.

All theories of value, price mechanisms, and resource allocation are, at bottom, answering one question: **who gets the limited thing, and how is it divided?**

What AI is breaking through is exactly the "scarcity" end. But note: AI does not break through all scarcity—only **specific types**: everything that can be digitized, patternized, and mass-replicated.

This is not the end of economics. It is a **foundational displacement**. Scarcity doesn't disappear; it migrates. The core question shifts from *"how do we allocate limited resources"* to *"what is genuinely limited under artificial abundance?"*

This outline tries to answer: **Where does scarcity migrate? What is the new theory of value? How does power reorganize? Where is the way out for ordinary people?**

---

## 1 · The Migration Map of Scarcity

Every technological revolution doesn't eliminate scarcity. It migrates it. Material abundance does not mean scarcity disappears. It means scarcity **moves**.

### 1.1 Scarcity That Is Already Migrating

| Old scarcity (Industrial Age) | New scarcity (AI Age) |
|---|---|
| Food, goods | Attention |
| Information | Trustworthy information (a sense of the real) |
| Execution capacity (labor) | Judgment (meaning production) |
| Transportation/communication links | Real human connection |
| Survival time | Quality conscious time |

![Scarcity moves: old scarcity vs new scarcity](/images/posts/ai-economics-outline/scarcity-moves.svg)
*Figure: Scarcity doesn't disappear—it moves. From goods, information, and labor, to attention, judgment, and real human connection.*

### 1.2 Things About to Become Scarce

**① Verifiable human provenance**  
Once AI-generated content floods everything, "this was created by a human" becomes a scarce label in itself.  
Like organic food or handmade goods—not because it works better, but because the **source is scarce**.

**② Legitimacy**  
Who has the authority to define what is real, valuable, and trustworthy?  
In an information explosion, **authorization itself becomes a scarce resource**.

**③ Sovereignty over attention**  
A finite 24 hours × a finite cognitive bandwidth.  
The richer you are, the more your focus time is worth. The poorer you are, the more cheaply your attention is harvested.  
What is truly scarce is not just attention, but **the autonomous judgment that isn't pushed around by algorithms**.

**④ Body and lifespan**  
Information can be copied infinitely. A body cannot.  
Health, longevity, cognitive clarity—this is the domain AI cannot equalize.

**⑤ Real human relationships and community belonging**  
Once AI companions flood the market, "this person actually cares about me" becomes a luxury.  
Not the flood of "social media followers," but a network of deep relationships that truly witness and answer to each other.  
Social trust, belonging, being truly seen—none of it can be algorithmized.

**⑥ Meaning frameworks**  
Not information, not entertainment, but **a narrative structure that gives life a sense of direction**.  
This is humanity's deepest need, and it will be the hardest scarcity to satisfy.

**⑦ Privacy and the freedom not to be watched**  
In a default world of total surveillance, real privacy becomes an extreme luxury.  
The ability to "disappear," to live unwatched, will become the new standard equipment of the ultra-rich.

**⑧ Physical places and natural experience**  
The digital world can be copied infinitely. The physical world cannot.  
Untouched landscapes, the physical presence of a particular city, face-to-face bodily co-presence—since the pandemic, the premium people pay for "being together for real" has risen sharply. This is just the beginning.

## 2 · The Value-Collapse Map: What AI Is Mass-Producing

In the following domains, supply tends toward infinity and price tends toward zero:

### Cognitive labor (the core)
- Text production: press releases, reports, emails, basic analysis
- Code writing: standardized engineering work
- Translation: language barriers vanish
- Design: basic visuals, UI/UX
- Data processing: cleaning, analysis, visualization

### Content and media
- The cost of producing images, video, and music → 0
- Personalized content can be custom-generated infinitely
- The marginal cost of educational content → 0 (one AI can teach everyone on Earth)

### Information itself
- Knowledge is no longer a barrier (anyone can instantly get expert-level answers)
- The signaling value of credentials and certificates depreciates quickly
- "Knowing the answer" stops being valuable; "asking the right question" becomes valuable

### Basic services
- The standardized parts of customer service, diagnosis, and consulting
- Logistics planning and optimization
- Financial modeling

**Conclusion: every form of "replicable cognition" faces value zeroing out within the next decade.**

## 3 · A New Theory of Value: From Labor to Provenance

### 3.1 The End of the Labor Theory of Value

Marx's labor theory of value: a commodity's value comes from the socially necessary labor time congealed within it.  
The problem: when AI compresses labor time to nearly zero, does the commodity still have value?

Answer: **In the functional dimension, value approaches zero. In the meaning dimension, value is redistributed.**

### 3.2 The Provenance Theory of Value

**The new source of value: not "how much work went in," but "who made it, when, and under what conditions."**

This is already happening:
- The same wine, priced 10x apart because the winery's story differs
- The same painting, priced 100x apart because of the artist's life experience
- The same code, valued differently because a legendary engineer wrote it

In the AI age, this logic extends to everything:  
**Provenance is value. Narrative is pricing power.**

### 3.3 Beyond the Attention Economy: Judgment as the Core Scarcity

The current mainstream view: attention is the core scarcity, and the economy reorganizes around the fight for it.  
That is correct, but not deep enough.

**The deeper scarcity is sovereignty over attention—that is, autonomous judgment.**

Whoever controls attention controls decisions.  
But active, autonomous judgment—the ability not to be pushed around by algorithms—  
will become the most expensive human capability of the 21st century.  
What the rich are paying for is not just products, but **the right not to be disturbed by recommendations**.

## 4 · The Meaning Economy: Beyond the Experience Economy

Pine & Gilmore's (1999) value migration chain:  
**products → goods → services → experiences → transformations**

AI will commodify experience itself. Any experience can be simulated, mass-produced, recommended.

**The genuinely scarce layer below: meaning.**

### 4.1 Why Meaning Cannot Be Mass-Produced by AI

Viktor Frankl: humanity's deepest driver is not pleasure but meaning.

The essential features of meaning:
- It must be discovered by oneself; it cannot be given from outside
- It requires real struggle, failure, and choice—none of which AI can do on your behalf
- It depends on real relationships with others, not AI

A life perfectly arranged by AI is meaningless—because you never actually chose anything.

### 4.2 What the Meaning Economy Looks Like

The meaning economy doesn't sell products or experiences. It sells:  
**"Who you are, why you are here, and what weight your existence carries."**

- **Ritual and tradition**: real cultural festivals, coming-of-age ceremonies, collective narratives—time anchors that cannot be algorithmized
- **The commercialization of difficulty**: outdoor challenges, elite training—trading real hardship for a sense of meaning
- **Paid community belonging**: joining a real human community with an entry threshold
- **Life narrative design**: helping people construct and tell the story of their own life

### 4.3 The Consciousness Economy: The Ultimate Scarcity

One step deeper: when AI can simulate almost everything, the only thing it cannot replicate is **consciousness itself**—  
the inner feeling of subjective experience, "what it is like to be you" (qualia).

The ultimate scarce good of the future is **a real, conscious, present human being**.

Early signals are already here:
- People pay for "listener" services
- People pay a high premium to "be truly seen"
- The meditation and mindfulness industry is exploding
- "Human companionship" is emerging as a premium service

## 5 · New Veblen Goods and the New Status Hierarchy

Veblen goods: the higher the price, the higher the demand, because their essence is a **status signal**.

### 5.1 Old Status Symbols in Decline

- Luxury cars (once self-driving is everywhere, they stop being a display of "I can handle this machine")
- Designer handbags (AI design + robotic production strips luxury of production scarcity)
- Information advantage ("I know something you don't"—cognitive equalization after AI)

### 5.2 New Status Symbols on the Rise

**① Verifiable human-made goods**  
"This furniture was made by a carpenter with a name, a story. You can talk to him."  
The revival of the artisan economy is not nostalgia—it is identity consumption.

**② Time that cannot be disturbed**  
What the rich are buying: blank time with no push notifications, no alerts, no algorithmic recommendations.  
Digital detox is already a luxury service. This is just the beginning.

**③ Exclusive access to real natural environments**  
Land will never increase. Undeveloped nature, quiet bays, clean air—  
when the virtual world is infinite, the physical world becomes the ultimate luxury.

**④ Exclusive access to human time**  
Private doctors, private chefs, private tutors—  
not because of the service itself, but because **a real human is devoting full attention to you**.  
When AI services are everywhere, the focus of human time becomes scarce in itself.

**⑤ Cultural identity and provenance**  
"I am the inheritor of a family/tradition/culture"—an irreproducible historical depth.  
The economic logic of aristocracy revives in a new form: **exclusive pricing of identity**.

**⑥ Early access to cognitive enhancement**  
Whoever gets the best brain-computer interfaces, cognitive optimization tools, and precision education algorithms first—  
the gap in knowledge acquisition speed will produce a **cognitive class**, the hardest new class barrier to cross.

### 5.3 Breaking the New Status Down Into Six Kinds of Capital

When matter is abundant, what do people use to show off, compete, and express superiority? Six layers:

1. **Biological capital (the ultimate luxury)**: lifespan, health optimization, looks and physical ability—the highest-end good money can buy directly
2. **Attention capital (the new power)**: whose words get heard and spread—not follower counts, but real depth of influence
3. **Privacy capital (the new freedom)**: the ability to live outside surveillance, uncaptured by algorithms, with time not occupied by platforms
4. **Meaning capital (the new religion)**: having a clear purpose and mission, and being able to give others a meaning framework
5. **Relationship capital (the new social status)**: a network of genuinely deep human relationships—not "followers," but "witnesses"
6. **Physical-experience capital**: where you have been, what rare experiences you have had, the depth and breadth of bodily feeling

The first two layers can be commercialized and scaled. The latter four are essentially non-commercial—but new commercial ecosystems will grow around them.

## 6 · The New Economic Laws

### Law One: The Law of Scarcity Migration
Every productivity revolution does not eliminate scarcity; it migrates it. Value follows scarcity.

### Law Two: The Law of Meaning Inflation
Once material needs are met, the "price" of meaning needs rises exponentially.  
Maslow's hierarchy becomes a market law: once lower needs are satisfied, upper needs become the new market.

### Law Three: Veblen Amplification
Traditional Veblen effect: higher prices sell better (expensive = status).  
The AI version: **inefficiency itself becomes a luxury marker.**
- Handmade > machine-made
- Human writing > AI-generated
- Slow food > fast food
- Inconvenience = premium

The more advanced AI becomes, the more valuable human "inefficiency" gets.

### Law Four: The Law of Attention Conservation
Total human attention is a constant. When content supply tends toward infinity, the scarce nodes that actually capture attention become astronomically valuable.

### Law Five: The Rising Authenticity Premium
The more forgeable a domain is, the higher the premium on verifiable authenticity.  
This is the AI age's "automatic scarcity-production mechanism."

### Law Six: The Permanence of Status Competition
No matter how high productivity goes, **relative status** is always a zero-sum game.  
Humans will always compete for rank along some dimension.  
The new question is not "will there be status competition," but "what will the currency of competition become."

### Law Seven: The Law of Minimum Viable Consent
When labor is no longer needed, the ruling class's need for the underclass changes into: the minimum political consent required to maintain social order.  
Policies like UBI are essentially **the cost of buying silence**, not redistributive justice.

## 7 · A New Anatomy of Power and Political Economy

### 7.1 Manufacturing Scarcity by Hand: The System's Self-Preservation Logic

**Core insight: material abundance does not automatically produce equality, because the ruling class actively manufactures new artificial scarcity.**

Historical pattern:
- The printing press made information cheap → aristocrats monopolized educational institutions and the power of certification
- The Industrial Revolution made goods cheap → capitalists monopolized distribution channels
- The internet made content cheap → platforms monopolized attention distribution
- **AI makes cognitive labor cheap → ? What gets monopolized?**

In the AI age, the new forms of artificial scarcity:
- **Monopoly on certification**: who has the authority to define "this AI/this person is trustworthy"
- **Control of physical space**: land, urban access, freedom of movement
- **Control of biological capital**: access to longevity technology, gene editing, cognitive enhancement
- **The power to grant legitimacy**: whose voice the algorithm amplifies, whose identity the system recognizes

### 7.2 The New Currency of Power: Legitimacy

Money is, in essence, **a quantification of social trust**.  
When matter is no longer scarce, society needs a new medium of exchange.

The real currency of power will be: **the extent to which you are considered trustworthy and worth following by how many people.**  
That is, an exchangeable form of legitimacy.

### 7.3 The Fate of Three Kinds of People

**① The owners/designers of algorithms (0.01%)**  
Those who control the flow of attention, the ranking of value, the filtering of information.  
These are the "printing press owners" of the new age. Very few, extremely powerful.

**② Producers/gatekeepers of meaning (1-5%)**  
Cultural authorities, community builders, narrative architects, institution designers.  
In an age of scarce meaning, these people hold real social influence.  
The oldest form of power in history, resurrected in a new face.

**③ The managed masses**  
Given basic security (UBI-style), stripped of the meaning of work, falling into a meaning vacuum, becoming targets for attention harvesting.  
Unless—they find a way out of their own.

## 8 · Macro Structure: The Post-Labor Economy

### 8.1 The Meaning Crisis of GDP
Traditional GDP measures the monetary value of goods and services.  
When AI drives marginal cost → 0, the monetized form of a great deal of real value disappears.  
GDP could keep falling while the economy is "prospering"—creating a massive tear between statistical systems and reality.

**New dimensions are needed:**
- Attention quality index
- Meaning satisfaction
- Relationship depth index
- Biological health capital

### 8.2 The Changing Role of Money
Money shifts from "medium of exchange" to "certificate of scarcity."
New "money" forms:
- Verified human reputation (unforgeable reputation points)
- Attention tokens (your time and focus are scarce and can be explicitly traded)
- Ownership of biological data

### 8.3 The Final Form of the Platform Economy
Platforms no longer merely connect buyers and sellers; they **manage meaning, attention, and community belonging**.  
The platform that controls the largest meaning container holds religion-like power.

### 8.4 The Political Economy of UBI
UBI is not charity. It is **the cost of minimum viable social stability**.  
The question is not "whether to give it," but "how much is just enough to keep people from revolting without letting them accumulate the capacity to resist."  
Those who accept UBI trade consumption freedom for political silence and data contributions.

## 9 · Historical Analogy: How Scarcity Migrates

| Era | Core productivity revolution | Destroyed scarcity | New scarcity | Winners | Losers |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Agricultural Revolution | Farming techniques | Foraging ability | Land | Feudal lords | Free peasants |
| Industrial Revolution | Machines/steam | Physical labor | Capital (machine ownership) | Capitalists, retailers | Traditional artisans |
| Electrification | Electricity/assembly line | Handcraft manufacturing | Organizational scale | Large-enterprise organizers | Artisan workshops |
| Information Revolution | Computers/internet | Cost of information distribution | Network nodes/platforms | Tech giants | Traditional media, retail |
| **AI Revolution** | **Generative AI/robots** | **Cognitive labor** | **Authenticity, meaning, legitimacy, biological capital** | **? Early positioners** | **Cognitive laborers** |

The pattern is clear: **the scarcity destroyed by a new technology is the source of the old power. The new scarcity is the source of the new power.**

In the Industrial Revolution, the biggest losers were artisans and land aristocrats (relatively); the biggest winners were factory owners and organizers. In the AI revolution, the biggest losers will be cognitive laborers; who the biggest winners are is not yet fully clear—that is the window.

## 10 · Transition Dynamics: Who Wins, Who Loses

### The peculiarity of a transition
A transition is not any steady state. It is a chaotic condition in which two sets of rules are simultaneously valid and mutually contradictory. The old rules aren't dead yet; the new rules aren't established. This is **the period of highest class mobility in history**, and also the riskiest. The window lasts roughly 10-20 years: the new class-solidification mechanisms haven't been built, and upward channels are relatively open. Afterward, the new elite will lock the channels with new rules, just as capitalists after the Industrial Revolution locked in their advantage through property law.

### Who necessarily loses during the transition
- The middle cognitive layer (the mass market of knowledge workers)
- Middlemen whose power rests on "information asymmetry"
- Traditional industries whose barriers are built on licenses and certificates

### Who unexpectedly gains during the transition
- Translators who can connect the old and new systems
- Stabilizers who can manage the chaos of the transition
- Early movers who establish positions in the new scarcity domains
- Providers who can produce meaning, trust, and a sense of the real

## 11 · The Escape Map: Real Opportunities for Ordinary People in the AI Economy

> What follows is not a motivational list. It is structural analysis. Every path has real obstacles and real possibilities.

### Opportunity One: Become a "Transition Translator" (Most urgent, shortest window: 3-8 years)

**Essence**: people who understand both how old systems operate and what new AI tools can do are extremely scarce right now.

Large numbers of old systems (governments, traditional enterprises, hospitals, schools) need someone to help them:
- Judge which parts can be replaced by AI
- Design transition plans (not technical plans—human plans)
- Manage people's resistance and fear through the process

**You don't need to be the best programmer.** You need to understand organizations, human nature, and what AI is actually doing.  
Once the window closes, this knowledge gets encoded into standard consulting services and the premium disappears. **Right now is the moment of maximum premium.**

### Opportunity Two: Occupy the "Authenticity" Node (Mid-term, highly durable)

**Essence**: when AI content floods everything, "this was really made/lived/expressed by a human" becomes a scarce label.

Concrete forms:
- **Creators with verifiable stories**: not the highest output, but the most real provenance
- **Consultants with real professional experience**: not the most material read, but people who have actually done it, failed, and carry scars
- **Organizers of real communities**: not the most followers, but real human relationships inside the community

**Core moat**: the incompressibility of time. Your real experience cannot be copied by AI at accelerated speed.  
The earlier you start accumulating real experience and leaving a verifiable trail, the higher the barrier.

### Opportunity Three: Build "Meaning Infrastructure" (Long-term, high risk, high return)

**Essence**: in the post-work era, people's deepest need is meaning. Whoever provides meaning frameworks holds the strongest influence and commercial value.

This is the oldest business in history: religion, philosophical schools, cultural movements.  
Modern forms:
- Communities with real values (a community with a worldview, not an interest group)
- Educational models that provide a "life operating system"
- The design of ritual and shared narrative

**Risk**: this path slides easily into manipulation (cult logic).  
The sustainable core is: **helping people find meaning themselves, not selling them yours.**

### Opportunity Four: Hold a "Trust Node" (Most stable)

**Essence**: in a world full of synthetic content and unknowable sources, trust itself becomes infrastructure.

Whoever holds a trust node in a network—where everyone is willing to verify, endorse, and transmit through you—holds the "key router" of the new age.

**Concrete forms**:
- Become the connector with a genuinely deep relationship network in a domain
- Not LinkedIn-style surface connections, but real mutual understanding
- Your endorsement carries weight; your introduction means something

**Key insight**: the value of a trust network is superlinear. Once you connect enough key nodes, your value doesn't grow linearly—it explodes through network effects.

### Opportunity Five: Invest Deeply in Body Capital (Individual level, most certain)

**Essence**: in a world of cheap information and harvested attention, a healthy body and clear cognition are the real scarce assets.

This is not consumerist wellness. It is strategic investment:
- **Cognitive capacity**: focus, memory, judgment (the most valuable human abilities in the AI age)
- **Physical endurance**: the ability to keep working when others are exhausted
- **Emotional stability**: the ability to stay clear-headed amid uncertainty

**Longevity will be the next great class divide.**  
In a world of equalized information, living longer and living better is the last material privilege.  
Start investing now. Don't wait until you have money. Enter health tech and longevity research—knowledge investment starts earlier than financial investment.

### Opportunity Six: Geographic and Systemic Arbitrage (High certainty, requires action)

**Essence**: the time lag in AI's impact across regions creates real arbitrage windows.

Two paths:
- **Go where the shock has not yet arrived**, and use AI capability to become locally scarce (a higher-dimensional advantage)
- **Go where the rules are being rebuilt**, and take part in the early design of the new order

In many developing countries and regions dense with traditional industries, AI penetration is extremely low.  
Someone who understands AI is a scarce resource there.

### Opportunity Seven: Become a "Coordinator," Not an "Executor" (The deepest repositioning)

**Essence**: AI is increasingly capable of handling individual tasks, but what it cannot replace is **getting multiple humans to move in the same direction**.

AI can: execute tasks, optimize processes, generate plans  
AI struggles to: build trust among emotional people, resolve conflict, inspire genuine commitment

Concrete abilities:
- Building trust between strangers
- Resolving conflict among emotional people
- Making people truly commit rather than go through the motions
- Maintaining a collective sense of direction in chaos

These abilities are not taught in schools, not needed in factories, but in the AI age they are **the last human monopoly skill**.  
Starting now, actively practice organizing and coordinating people in whatever environment you are in.

### The Most Important Insight About Breaking Out

**Don't try to win the old game. Position yourself in the new one.**

The old game's rule: trade more time and more effort for a better position.

The new game's rule: whoever understands first where the new scarcity lies can occupy it at low cost before the scarcity is widely recognized.

**The moment the crowd recognizes a scarcity is the moment you leave, not the moment you enter.**

## 12 · The Underlying Framework

```
Age of material scarcity
    Value = labor × time
    Power = who owns the means of production
    Status = display of consumption capacity

              ↓ AI rewrites the underlying logic

Age of meaning scarcity
    Value = provenance × authenticity × meaning density
    Power = who defines legitimacy + who distributes attention + who provides meaning frameworks
    Status = the unreplicable human (experience, relationships, consciousness, body)
```

## Conclusion

> In a world where AI mass-produces everything, your scarcity comes from your **incompressible human experience**.  
>  
> Time, body, real relationships, real struggle, verifiable provenance—  
> these are what AI cannot produce at accelerated speed.  
>  
> The earlier you start accumulating these irreplicable assets,  
> how far you can go before the window closes  
> depends on whether you see the new rules of the game clearly right now.

Don't ask, "What service can I provide?"  
Ask, "What will become scarcer in the AI age, and can I become the provider of that scarcity?"

Find a domain where **authenticity × meaning × trust** intersect, and build a position there that AI cannot replicate. Then, before the rules solidify, use the chaos of the transition as fuel for ascent.

What the builders of the system fear most is not the anger of the underclass, but the underclass understanding the rules of the game—and quietly completing its migration of position during the window when the rules are being rewritten.
